AI Regulation Comparison: India, EU, and US

Balancing Technological Innovation, Fundamental Human Rights, and Software Engineering Practice

Academic Capstone Research Scope & Core Problem

Artificial Intelligence systems operate on statistical probability rather than deterministic code. Non-deterministic ML models create acute societal risks: algorithmic demographic bias, privacy breaches, hallucinated medical outputs, and synthetic disinformation. Classical software liability shields no longer apply.

Primary Objective: Equip Computer Science Engineering (CSE) students, software engineers, and startups with a rigorous understanding of the divergent statutory frameworks across India, the EU, and the US to transition from naive prototyping to legally compliant production engineering.

European Union

Horizontal, precautionary, omnibus statute with strict extraterritorial enforcement (EU AI Act).

United States

Sectoral, agency-driven enforcement (FTC, EEOC, FDA), NIST benchmarks, and pioneering state laws.

India

Developmental hybrid model: Sovereign compute via IndiaAI Mission anchored by DPDPA 2023 & MeitY.

AI Regulations in India: The Innovation & Privacy Framework

India prioritizes domestic innovation and sovereign compute infrastructure while enforcing algorithmic safety through data protection and targeted administrative advisories.

1. Data Protection Anchor

Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA)

  • Mandatory itemized, informed consent for digital personal data.
  • Section 9 Child Safeguards: Complete ban on tracking or behavioral profiling of minors under 18 years.
  • Penalties up to ₹250 Crore (~$30M USD) adjudicated by the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI).

2. Administrative Guidance

MeitY Advisories (March 2024)

  • Issued under IT Rules, 2021 for intermediaries and platforms.
  • Mandatory public labeling of untested or experimental AI models.
  • Mandatory cryptographic watermarking and provenance metadata on synthetic deepfakes.
  • Strict prohibitions against electoral disruption or religious/caste bias.

3. Sovereign Capability

IndiaAI Mission (₹10,372 Cr)

  • Procuring 10,000+ subsidized GPUs for students, academia, and Indian startups.
  • IndiaAI Datasets Platform: Unified open public sector data across 22 scheduled languages.
  • Safe & Trusted AI Pillar establishing domestic red-teaming benchmarks.

EU AI Act: Global Benchmark for Risk-Based Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689: The world's first comprehensive horizontal AI statute, entering into force August 1, 2024, with worldwide extraterritorial effect.

1. UNACCEPTABLE RISK (Art. 5) — BANNED ENTIRELY
Social scoring, cognitive behavioral manipulation, untargeted facial scraping, workplace emotion analysis
Max Fine: €35M / 7% Turnover
2. HIGH RISK (Arts. 6–51, Annex III) — STRICT CONFORMITY & CE MARKING
Biometrics, recruitment/HR, education grading, clinical healthcare, critical infrastructure
Max Fine: €15M / 3% Turnover
3. SPECIFIC TRANSPARENCY RISK (Art. 50) — MANDATORY DISCLOSURE
Chatbots, interactive AI agents, deepfakes, synthetic audio/video generation
Machine Watermarks Required
4. MINIMAL / NO RISK — FREE UNRESTRICTED DEPLOYMENT
AI spam filters, video games, inventory forecasting, optimization algorithms
Voluntary Codes of Conduct

General Purpose AI (GPAI): Foundation models trained on > 1025 FLOPs carry systemic risk obligations: mandatory red-teaming, copyright compliance, energy efficiency reporting, and incident disclosure to the European AI Office.

US AI Governance: Decentralized Enforcement & Standards

The United States relies on sector-specific regulatory enforcement, technical standards from NIST, and state legislative momentum.

Federal Executive Actions

  • Executive Order 14110: Defense Production Act notification for foundation models > 1026 FLOPs.
  • OMB Memo M-24-10: Mandates Chief AI Officers and civil rights safeguards in federal agencies.
  • Voluntary safety commitments by leading frontier model labs.

Agency Enforcement

  • FTC Act Section 5: Prosecutes deceptive AI claims and orders algorithmic disgorgement (model destruction).
  • EEOC Title VII: Enforces the 80% Four-Fifths Rule against discriminatory hiring algorithms.
  • FDA SaMD: Regulates clinical AI through 510(k) and Predetermined Change Control Plans (PCCPs).

State-Level Statutes

  • Colorado SB 24-205: Affirmative duty of care to avoid algorithmic discrimination in high-risk AI.
  • California AB 2013 & SB 942: Training data transparency and synthetic watermarking mandates.
  • NYC Local Law 144: Mandatory annual independent bias audits for automated hiring tools.

Case Studies and Real-World Applications

1. Facial Attendance (FRAS)

Campus / Office Biometrics

  • EU: High Risk (Annex III) / Prohibited in public spaces (Art. 5). Requires explicit GDPR Art. 9 consent.
  • US: Illinois BIPA written consent; FTC bans on disproportionate false identification.
  • India: DPDPA 2023 consent; strict §9 ban on minor student tracking (<18 years).
  • Fix: Provide RFID alternatives & salt/encrypt vector embeddings.

2. Clinical Diagnostic App

Mobile Skin Lesion Classifier

  • EU: High Risk + Medical Device Regulation (MDR 2017/745) Notified Body CE approval.
  • US: FDA SaMD 510(k) premarket review; HIPAA data encryption; Predetermined Change Control.
  • India: CDSCO medical device licensing; mandatory MeitY experimental disclaimer.
  • Fix: Implement uncertainty thresholding & licensed doctor review gate.

3. Automated HR Screening

Resume Screening Engine

  • EU: High Risk (Annex III); GDPR Art. 22 ban on fully automated rejections.
  • US: NYC LL 144 annual bias audit; EEOC 80% Four-Fifths rule for disparate impact.
  • India: DPDPA purpose limitation; Constitutional equality under Articles 14 & 15.
  • Fix: Strip protected demographic attributes; automate disparate impact testing in CI/CD.

Conclusion, Dev Best Practices & Future Scope

The CSE Engineer's Responsible Dev-Stack

Ethical engineering is no longer an afterthought. In modern computer science, regulatory compliance must be integrated from sprint zero:

1. Dataset Provenance & Consent: Verify data sources, avoid copyrighted code in training without authorization, and audit for demographic fairness.

2. Synthetic Provenance: Integrate C2PA-compliant watermarking and user disclaimers for all generative outputs.

3. Telemetry & Auditing: Implement immutable inference logs, SHAP/LIME explainability tools, and automated regression tests.

4. Human Oversight Circuits: Always build Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) manual overrides for consequential decisions.
