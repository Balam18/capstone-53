### 1. Purpose of the Accuracy Checklist
In legal and regulatory research involving emerging technologies, Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently suffer from **temporal misalignment**, **jurisdictional conflation**, and **statutory hallucinations** (e.g., fabricating non-existent acts like a "US Federal AI Consumer Safety Act", claiming voluntary frameworks are criminal statutes, or quoting fictitious penalty figures).

This Accuracy Checklist provides a standardized, line-item verification instrument used throughout Capstone 53 to audit every statutory citation, regulatory risk tier, enforcement authority, and financial penalty against official gazettes and primary legislative portals.

### 3. Detailed Line-Item Verification by Jurisdiction

#### Part A: European Union (EU)
- [x] **Official Instrument:** *Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council* (EU AI Act).
- [x] **Publication Date:** Published in the Official Journal of the EU on July 12, 2024.
- [x] **Entry into Force:** Entered into force August 1, 2024.
- [x] **Phase-in Schedule:**
  - *6 Months (February 2, 2025):* Prohibitions on Unacceptable Risk practices (Article 5) take effect.
  - *12 Months (August 2, 2025):* Obligations for General Purpose AI (GPAI) governance and penalties become active.
  - *24 Months (August 2, 2026):* High-Risk AI systems under Annex III must fully comply.
  - *36 Months (August 2, 2027):* High-Risk AI systems embedded in regulated products (Annex I) must comply.
- [x] **Risk Categories Verified:**
  1. *Unacceptable Risk (Art. 5):* Subliminal manipulation, social scoring, biometric categorization for sensitive traits, predictive policing based purely on profiling, untargeted scraping of facial images from the web/CCTV.
  2. *High Risk (Arts. 6-51, Annex I & III):* Biometrics, critical infrastructure, educational access/grading, recruitment & HR, essential public services, law enforcement, immigration, judicial systems.
  3. *Specific Transparency Risk (Art. 50):* AI systems interacting directly with humans (chatbots), emotion recognition, biometric categorization, generative text/audio/video (deepfakes).
  4. *Minimal / Low Risk:* All other AI systems (spam filters, games, inventory forecasting) - free deployment.
- [x] **Penalties Verified (Art. 99):**
  - Prohibited AI violations: Up to €35,000,000 or 7% of worldwide annual turnover.
  - High-Risk AI non-compliance: Up to €15,000,000 or 3% of worldwide annual turnover.
  - Providing misleading information: Up to €7,500,000 or 1.5% of worldwide annual turnover.
  - *Note:* Lower thresholds apply for SMEs and startups.
- [x] **Enforcement Bodies Verified:** European AI Office (within the European Commission), European Data Protection Board (EDPB), and National Supervisory Authorities.

#### Part B: United States (US)
- [x] **Primary Federal Instruments:**
  - *Executive Order 14110:* Signed October 30, 2023, by President Joseph R. Biden Jr., invoking the Defense Production Act (50 U.S.C. 4501 et seq.) for foundation models trained on $>10^{26}$ FLOPs.
  - *OMB Memorandum M-24-10:* Issued March 28, 2024, binding federal agencies on Chief AI Officers, impact assessments, and risk mitigations.
- [x] **Technical Framework:** *NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, NIST SP 1270)*, published January 2023. Explicitly verified as **voluntary** for private industry, structured across GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE.
- [x] **Federal Enforcement Authorities:**
  - *Federal Trade Commission (FTC):* Section 5 of the FTC Act (15 U.S.C. § 45) prohibiting unfair or deceptive acts or practices; orders algorithmic disgorgement.
  - *Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC):* Enforces Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 regarding algorithmic selection tools creating disparate impact.
  - *Food and Drug Administration (FDA):* Regulates clinical AI under Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) through 510(k), De Novo, and Premarket Approval (PMA) pathways.
- [x] **State-Level Statutes Verified:**
  - *Colorado SB 24-205:* Consumer Protections for AI, enacted May 2024, taking effect 2026. Regulates "high-risk artificial intelligence systems" in consequential life decisions.
  - *California AB 2013:* Enacted September 2024. Mandates public disclosure of training dataset summaries by developers of generative AI systems.
  - *California SB 942:* California AI Transparency Act, enacted September 2024. Requires AI detection tools and watermarking of AI-generated content.
  - *NYC Local Law 144 of 2021:* Effective July 2023. Mandates independent annual bias audits for Automated Employment Decision Tools (AEDTs).

#### Part C: India
- [x] **Primary Statutes & Legal Instruments:**
  - *The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023):* Passed by Indian Parliament and notified August 11, 2023.
  - *Information Technology Act, 2000 & IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021.*
  - *MeitY Advisories on AI & Deepfakes:* Issued March 1, 2024; amended and clarified on March 15, 2024.
- [x] **Key Provisions Verified:**
  - *DPDPA Section 9:* Strict prohibition against tracking, behavioral monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at children (<18 years); requires verifiable parental consent.
  - *DPDPA Section 27 & Schedule 1:* Data Protection Board of India (DPBI) empowered to levy administrative penalties up to **₹250 Crore (approx. $30M USD)** for failure to implement reasonable security safeguards.
  - *MeitY March 15, 2024 Clarification:* Government clarified that the prior advisory requirement for explicit permission to deploy un-tested AI was targeted at significant intermediaries/platforms, not early-stage startups; all AI platforms must label synthetic outputs, watermark deepfakes, and ensure models do not exhibit bias or harm electoral integrity.
- [x] **National Infrastructure Initiative:** *IndiaAI Mission*, approved by Union Cabinet on March 7, 2024, with **₹10,372 Crore** budget allocation for 10,000+ subsidized GPUs, IndiaAI Datasets Platform, and IndiaAI Innovation Centre.
